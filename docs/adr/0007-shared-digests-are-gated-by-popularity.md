# Shared Digests are generated on demand for popular Subjects only

A Digest is produced one of two ways, decided by how much the Subject is being discussed:

- **Above a popularity threshold** (aggregate Discussion activity on HN and Reddit — score, comment count, number of Discussions): the Digest is a **Shared Digest**, generated on demand by our Cloudflare Worker, cached, and served to every reader of that page — including readers with no Provider connected.
- **Below the threshold**: the Digest is a **Local Digest**, generated on the reader's own machine by their own Provider, and it never leaves that machine.

Discussions keep growing after a Digest is written, so a Digest carries a **Watermark** — the state of the Discussions it was generated from. On a later request we compare current Discussion state against it: unchanged serves the existing Digest outright; changed enough triggers a rewrite.

**Amended 2026-08-08 — the Digest is a summary, not a stream of increments.** This ADR originally made the "delta" a reader-facing feature: *what's new since you last looked*. That is removed. A Digest is simply the current summary of the whole of a Subject's Discussions, rewritten as they grow, and **the reader always sees the current one and never a diff against an earlier one**.

The simplification deletes three things outright: the reader-facing **Delta**, the **Last Look** horizon, and the question of which horizon a Delta measures against. It also resolves the collision this ADR previously carried — that a Local→Shared crossover must be invisible while Shared Digests are "correctable" — because with no diff surfaced there is nothing for the crossover to reveal. The Watermark survives as **internal machinery only**: it decides when to rewrite, and is never shown or described to the reader as a horizon they possess.

## Why gate on popularity

It makes our AI spend bounded and self-limiting rather than proportional to users, and it puts the spend exactly where it amortizes best — one generation serving thousands of readers. It also repairs most of the gap left by [ADR 0004](./0004-ai-is-an-upgrade-not-a-dependency.md): a reader with no ChatGPT plan still gets Digests on the pages the most people are actually reading.

Because the Shared Digest is generated by us from public Discussions, **no user-authored content ever enters the shared corpus**. Poisoning is structurally impossible, and there is no moderation queue, no provenance UI, and no consent flow to build.

## Consequences

- We pay for Shared Digests. The threshold is the cost dial, and it must be tunable **from the static artifacts without shipping a build**, so spend can be controlled in response to reality.
- A Subject can cross the threshold over time. The same page can serve a Local Digest today and a Shared Digest tomorrow — the client must handle a Digest changing origin without the reader noticing anything except that it got faster. With no diff surfaced there is nothing for the crossover to reveal, so this is now a precedence rule rather than a design problem: whichever Digest is current wins.
- Change-detection needs a cheap, reliable signal of "have the Discussions moved" that does not require re-fetching every comment. Score and comment-count deltas from the Networks' list endpoints are the obvious candidates.
- The Watermark comparison is now purely a cost and freshness mechanism. Nothing about it is surfaced to the reader.
- Shared Digests are public artifacts of an open-source project: inspectable, reproducible from public Discussions, and correctable by issue. That auditability is a stated feature, not an implementation detail.
